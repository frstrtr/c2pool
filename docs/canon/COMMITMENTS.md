# Public commitments: the reply to the external review, held by the canon

On 2026-10-04 the operator (frstrtr) posted a reply on the external review of the XMR lane payout design (SChernykh, gist 636eca0b, comment 6406130). The reply admits two mistakes and makes public promises about the design. This file records every promise of that reply as rules of the design canon (`docs/canon/CANON.yaml`), together with the Path B rulings of 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-05 that make the promises precise (operator, 2026-10-05: "lets add provable work fees and other from my Schernykh gist reply in full to canon").

For every sentence that promises something, or that states a fact about the code which the canon must hold, the register gives the canon rules that hold the design to it, the status of those rules on master (frstrtr/c2pool b6e094c3, `python3 tools/canon/canon_check.py`), and where the design has moved since the reply was posted. A failing rule names its open deviation in `docs/canon/DEVIATIONS.md` and the slice that closes it. Nothing in this file is built yet unless its rule passes.

Rules added with this file: C36 (open carriage), C37 (payout from pre-block state), C38 (payout window by work, no output below its spend cost), C39 (the reviewer scenarios are required tests), C40 (design and simulation before code). Rules restated: C03, C13, C18 (still proposed), C19. Statements amended to match the rulings: C02, C12, C20, C22, C25, C26, C33. Deviations opened: D18 to D23; D2, D3, D7 and D9 updated.

Slices named here are those of the plan of 2026-10-05: S0 spec, canon and register (no code); S1 carrier sharechain; S2 receipts v3, open carriage, LEGALLY DEAD, tip freshness and the per-receipt coinbase check; S3 window, fold, MMR, exact split, N rule and W_max; S4 ledger removal and the ratchet re-cut; S5 raindrops; S6 payout proof and the owner-fee split; S7 towing.

## 1. The reply, verbatim

> You're right, and your numbers leave little room to argue.
>
> I had it backwards. I thought the owed ledger let a bounded coinbase pay an unbounded number of miners. What it really does is pay some miners too much in one block and promise the rest later, and nothing paid too much ever comes back. My last reply said the case doesn't come up. It does, every time the cap or the queue binds. That was wrong.
>
> There is a second mistake, and it is mine too. The Work Receipts design never needed a ledger to count late work. A share that loses the race is carried by the miner's own next share as a receipt, at full credit, and only for a couple of Monero blocks. The Monero code drifted from that. It has no carrier chain, it pays receipts for an hour after their template went stale, and it pays from an anchor 61 heights back, which is where your hopper gets 1.31. Each fix I made after that patched a symptom of the drift.
>
> So the ledger goes. Each pool block pays its own window in full, in its own coinbase, from state fixed before the block. Work on a Monero height the chain has already passed earns nothing, the same as an orphan block. Every miner in the window gets an output, and the window is sized so they fit. Your 20% miner with 40,000 small ones, and your pulse and hopper schedules, go into the tests as written.
>
> What is left open, plainly: without the ledger a very small miner waits about as long as on P2Pool, until the pool is big enough to split. A split is only safe if it doesn't hand a minority attacker a small target. And none of this is built yet.
>
> Where I think it can go further than what exists: work doesn't have to be ordered to be counted, so an orphaned share loses nothing and there is no uncle penalty. I am testing a split where a share's own proof of work decides which roundabout it lands in, so an attacker can't pick one without throwing work away. And a miner should be able to check, per job, that the coinbase it is hashing pays it the right amount, and check any node fee from the receipts themselves rather than take a percentage on trust.
>
> The 51% question is the one I'm researching hardest. If a share's own proof of work decides which roundabout it lands in, after it is found, every roundabout holds the same attacker fraction as the whole pool. Taking one roundabout then costs a majority of the whole pool, not of one part, and an attacker who wants to aim has to throw away every share that lands elsewhere. Selfish share mining should also stop paying, since the honest shares it orphans come back as receipts at full credit. A majority of the whole pool can still do what it can on P2Pool or on any chain; I don't claim otherwise. This is being simulated and modeled now; it is not a result yet.
>
> I'll post the design and the simulation before I write the code. If you have time to look at it then, I'd be grateful.

## 3. Where the design has moved since the reply: four notes

(a) "Earns nothing." Work on a passed Monero height is worth 0 once the chain has recorded the newer height, judged at the work's own position + 2 (C22). Before the chain records it, a stale template still earns: a template one Monero height behind keeps about 8-15 % of its work (0.148 +- 0.002 when it hangs on the newest compatible tip in the rbsim engine; 0.077 +- 0.007 in the hub model, where the next carrier places it); two heights behind it keeps 0.029 +- 0.001 (verify run of 2026-10-04/05, VERIFY.md section 2). Today's 30-bin tail pays it 1.00. Tip freshness (rulings 3 and 3') refuses receipts more than two heights ahead of their tip but does not stop origin-bin backdating; backdating is mining stale templates, useless work priced at block-withholding economics. The claim is therefore stated as "about a tenth of a lazy node's stale work rides, and none of it once the chain has recorded the new height", not as "earns nothing".

(b) "Only for a couple of Monero blocks." The carriage window is now F = 48 Monero heights (about 96 minutes, about 576 carrier positions; ruling R1), and it is the only lateness bound (ruling 1: no hop bound). Liveness is judged at the work's own position + 2 (own position = position of its tip + 1), so lateness of CARRIAGE no longer equals staleness of WORK: an honest share orphaned by a selfish release or a partition stays live and is credited in full at its origin bin whenever it is carried inside the open bin. Evidence: a 3 h minority partition recovers 0 % of its work with a hop bound of 8 and 54 % with none at F = 48 (98 % at F = 96); with a 2-bin carriage window a carriage-refusing selfish attacker at a = 0.45 gets 1.317, at F = 48 0.986.

(c) "Until the pool is big enough to split." v1 runs one carrier chain with K = 1 payment group and the N rule (ruling 8): no split, and every payee in the window is paid in every block, the window bounded by N(B) = min(N_param, 3/4 C_eff(B)) (2,676 today, about 1,248 after Carrot). Small miners get raindrops (slice S5, C18, ruling 7), not a split. Below a pool share of f_spend / R (about 1/48,000, 0.0021 %) any per-block payout is dust whatever the credit rule; raindrops serve miners between that and about 3 shares per window. Evidence: at equal expectation the N rule pays every class more often than payment groups (tiny miners 0.130 against 0.096 payouts a day, large 2.845 against 0.733).

(d) "A share's own proof of work decides which roundabout." Under that assignment every roundabout holds the attacker fraction of the whole pool (capture needs more than half of the pool; rbdefense T1 to T5), so a chain split buys no payout benefit; v1 therefore runs one chain (C12 stays proposed for a later multi-chain epoch). Payment groups K > 1 come later and use the FINDER's group: a block pays the group its finder belongs to, amounts w_i R / W_c. The prev_id group draw is deleted, because it makes (K-1)/K of the blocks pay their finder nothing, so withholding them is free. K > 1 is activated only on evidence (after Carrot, if the N rule shortens the window beyond a stated target, a run compares the finder's-group draw with the N rule) and by a ratchet vote (C23).

## 5. Owed, and not yet a rule

- Ruling C (every lane constant has a derivation) is ruled but not yet a canon rule: the operator sent it to a derivation run (F, T, d_min, E_h and an audit of every lane-rules field) before S0. The rule and its checker land with that run.
- R_MAX = 16 owes windows below 1.2 h (a pool above about 5.5 % of Monero) before the pool reaches that size, and the saturation sweep at R_MAX 16.
- W_max owes, in S0, the bound for entries of a smaller d than the tip (hashrate growth) and for raindrop entries (d/64), so that no payee output falls below f_spend, with its KAT (C38).
- Tip freshness: the honest cost of d <= 2 under real latency is measured in the S2 KAT and soak.
- C37, open S0 item (not ruled): which Monero branch D_net(h) and R(h) come from. DESIGN.md section 2.7 says only "the Monero header at h"; during a Monero race honest nodes could compute different windows, and C37 bans on a coinbase mismatch. Candidate for S0: take them from the branch of the receipt's own prev_id, which is deterministic; a node that lacks that branch defers and never bans (the existing "we cannot judge this right now: defer, never ban" rule, `src/impl/xmr/native/relay/xmr_found_block_carrier.hpp:39-43`). Source: the constants sweep, item A-3.
- C37, open S0 item (not ruled): coinbase output order. Carrot (Monero v17) requires coinbase outputs sorted by one-time output key, which conflicts with DESIGN.md section 2.7 "payee key ascending"; the canonical coinbase must follow the Monero output ordering rule of the block version. Source: the constants sweep, item A-9.
- Raindrops ship only after the ship gate of ruling 7 (C18).
- The register rows D18 to D23 and the updates of D2, D3, D7 and D9 are mirrored in frstrtr/the on the day this file merges.
